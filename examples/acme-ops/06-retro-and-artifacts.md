# Retro and artifacts, The Monday Number

## Retro, fifteen minutes, same call or next morning

What worked. What did not. What changes for the next event. Priya answers last.

## Reasoning card, one per pair

Result: the stand, in one sentence. Reasoning: three to five sentences in the pair's own words on why, including what they got wrong before Exhibit 2. Seed: the Copilot prompt or Google Sheets formula they actually used, written so the next person can edit it rather than copy it.

## Revised SOP skeleton, weekly pipeline review report

1. Sunday 6pm, automatic. HubSpot open deals view exported to Google Sheets on a schedule.
2. Sunday 6pm, automatic. A formula flags rows where the HubSpot stage and the notes stage disagree. No names leave the sheet.
3. Monday 7:30am, Luis or Dana, whoever is in. One batched Slack message to reps listing their flagged deal ids only, with a reply deadline of 8:30.
4. Monday 8:30am, Copilot drafts the expected close and slipped deals tabs from the updated sheet. Dana or Luis reviews every number against the sheet.
5. Monday 8:45am, the reviewer writes the commentary. Copilot may draft, the person signs.
6. Monday 9:00am, sent to the VP's inbox and posted to Slack.

Human checkpoints: steps 3, 4, and 5. Owner: Priya names one, with a named backup for the week either person is out.

## The delta

The intake's process had two people working in sequence for four hours with the slowest step upstream of the report. The revised process moves the export and the mismatch detection to Sunday night, batches the rep chase into one message with a deadline, and has AI draft the two tabs the VP reads while a person checks them. The change the manager can hold is the send time on the next four Mondays. That is the adoption evidence, and it keeps working after the last event.
