# The Sequence Nobody Owns, live session

## The full brief

Marcus Bell, Marketing Manager at Northwind Logistics Software, presents a fix to the CRO at the end of the month for a reply rate that fell from 4 percent to 1.8 percent across twelve HubSpot outbound sequences. The choice on the table is rewrite all twelve or change how they are reviewed. The team has HubSpot, Slack, Google Sheets, Copilot, and Notion. Claims about delivery times or savings need legal sign-off. No prospect data from outside HubSpot. No new tool, no more volume, no copywriter. Your pair's job is to tell Marcus what to do, as if he is walking into the room at minute ninety.

## Timeline, ninety minutes

Minutes 0 to 10, restate and clarify. Joint stands or hypotheses aloud. Facilitator answers from the intake only.

Minutes 10 to 20, structure. Three to five buckets. Cautious partners name what they would ask before changing anything that goes to a prospect. Confident partners name the review step that could run on its own and who signs.

Minutes 20 to 40, build, first gate. Exhibit 1 in full is released to any pair that asks for reply rate by sequence or by audience.

Minutes 40 to 55, second gate. Exhibit 2 is released to any pair that asks what changed on the sending side, or asks why the deliverability team capped sends.

Minute 55, Injection 1.

Minutes 55 to 70, build.

Minute 70, Injection 2.

Minutes 70 to 80, build.

Minutes 80 to 90, stands.

## Exhibit 2, the sending side, from the deliverability team

Seven weeks ago the cold sequences were moved to a new sending domain to protect the main domain. The new domain was not warmed. Bounce rate on cold sends went from 1.4 percent to 6.1 percent in the second week and spam complaints doubled. The deliverability team capped daily sends at 300 to stop the damage and posted about it in an engineering Slack channel the marketing team is not in. Warm and re-engage sequences still go from the main domain.

## Injection 1, minute 55

Marcus's message, read aloud: "Legal just came back on the VP Ops cost savings sequence. The 30 percent savings claim has to go, or we need a source for it by Friday." Pairs decide what happens to that sequence and to the review step that let the claim through.

## Injection 2, minute 70

Marcus's message, read aloud: "The new SDR asked me this morning how to use Copilot to personalize the cold sequences faster. What do I tell her?" This is the teaching question. A plan that fixes the sequences and leaves the newest person on the team to figure out the tool alone is a plan that repeats in three months.

## What would you do

Each pair, two minutes, to Marcus. The one sentence stand. Then: rewrite, review, or both, and in what order. Which step AI does and which step a person signs. Who owns the review step from now on. What you would pilot before the CRO meeting and how you would know it worked. The one risk you would tell the CRO about. A stand, not a menu.
